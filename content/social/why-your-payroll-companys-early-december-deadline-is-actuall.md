## LinkedIn

A 14-person dental office in Port Arthur hands out a $1,500 holiday bonus by manual check on December 30. Payroll never hears about it.

In February, the bookkeeper spots the check on the bank statement. Now the office needs a W-2c, a W-3c, an amended Form 941-X, an awkward conversation with an employee who may have already filed, and possibly penalties that start at $60 per incorrect return.

That is why your payroll company's early-December cutoff is closer to a smoke detector than a nuisance. W-2s are due January 31, and the IRS compares those totals against your four quarterly 941s. Catching a bonus, a 401(k) deferral change, or a taxable fringe benefit before the final payroll posts is far cheaper than fixing it after.
