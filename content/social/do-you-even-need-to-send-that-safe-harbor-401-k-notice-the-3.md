## LinkedIn

A dental practice with $580,000 in eligible payroll can fund a 3% nonelective safe harbor 401(k) for $17,400 and skip the December 1 notice entirely.

Here is the part plan providers rarely mention. A safe harbor plan that uses a matching contribution still has to send an annual notice, 30 to 90 days before the plan year starts. A plan built on a 3% nonelective contribution does not. The SECURE Act removed the notice for that design.

The cost trade-off is real. In the worked example, the 4% basic match runs $14,000 when only the owner and two staff defer, but $23,200 when everyone does. The nonelective stays at $17,400 either way. Miss the 30-day cutoff before year end and the price moves to 4%, which is $23,200.

For physicians, dentists, and veterinarians in Southeast Texas, the design you pick decides which calendar you live on. Your plan document controls, so confirm the details with your plan provider or TPA before acting.
